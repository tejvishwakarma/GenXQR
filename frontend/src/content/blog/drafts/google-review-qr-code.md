Most satisfied customers would leave a review if you asked, but very few go looking for the review box on their own. On Google, that means searching for your business, opening the profile, scrolling to reviews and tapping "Write a review". Every one of those steps loses people.

A Google review QR code skips them all: scan, and the review box opens with your business already selected.

## Step 1: get your review link

You need the link that opens your business's review box. There are two ways to get it:

- **From your Google Business Profile.** Sign in, open your profile and choose **Ask for reviews** (sometimes shown as **Get more reviews**). Google gives you a short link to copy.
- **From your Place ID.** Every business on Google Maps has a Place ID, a code starting with `ChIJ`. You can look yours up with Google's Place ID Finder.

GenXQR accepts either one. Paste the link or the Place ID, and the code takes scanners straight to your review box.

## Step 2: make it a dynamic code

Create a **Google Review** QR code in your GenXQR dashboard. Because it is dynamic, you can:

- **Count the scans,** so you can see whether the standee at the counter or the card in the bag gets more people to the review box
- **Fix the link later** if your profile changes, without reprinting
- **Give each branch its own code** and add it as a separate place, if you have more than one outlet

## Step 3: put it where happy customers are

Timing matters more than design. Ask at the moment the customer is pleased:

- **On the bill folder or receipt** at a restaurant or cafe
- **At the reception desk** of a salon, clinic or gym, as people leave
- **On the delivery packaging** or a thank-you card in the parcel
- **On your invoice or in a WhatsApp message** after a job is done (send the review link itself in messages; the QR is for print)

Add a short, plain request next to the code: "Enjoyed your visit? Scan to review us on Google."

## Step 4: say it out loud

A QR code on the counter works better when a person mentions it. A simple "If you liked it, a Google review really helps us" at the end of a good visit is usually enough.

## The rules to follow

Google's review policies are strict, and breaking them can get reviews removed. In short:

- **Don't pay for reviews or offer anything in return,** such as a discount or a free item for leaving a review.
- **Ask everyone, not only the customers you think are happy.** Steering only satisfied customers to Google while sending unhappy ones elsewhere goes against Google's guidelines.
- **Don't write reviews for your own business** or ask staff and family to.
- **Reply to reviews,** good and bad. People reading reviews notice how a business responds.

If you also want private feedback, keep it separate: a feedback form QR code on the table, offered to everyone, with the Google review code next to it.
