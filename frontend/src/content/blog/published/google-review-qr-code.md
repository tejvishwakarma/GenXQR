Most satisfied customers would leave a review if you asked, but very few go looking for the review box on their own. On Google, that means searching for your business, opening the profile, scrolling to reviews and tapping "Write a review". Every one of those steps loses people.

A Google review QR code skips them all: scan, and the review box opens with your business already selected.

## Why reviews are worth the effort

When someone searches for "cafe near me" or "dentist in Indiranagar", the businesses Google shows come with their star rating and the number of reviews, before anyone has clicked. A profile with a handful of old reviews looks quieter than one with a steady stream of recent ones, even if the service is the same. And people read the reviews themselves: what customers say about waiting times, prices or the staff often decides the visit.

You can't control what people write. You can make it easy for the customers who are happy to say so.

## Step 1: get your review link

You need the link that opens your business's review box. There are two ways to get it:

- **From your Google Business Profile.** Sign in, open your profile and choose **Ask for reviews** (sometimes shown as **Get more reviews**). Google gives you a short link to copy.
- **From your Place ID.** Every business on Google Maps has a Place ID, a code starting with `ChIJ`. You can look yours up with Google's Place ID Finder.

GenXQR accepts either one. Paste the link (Google's short `g.page` links, `maps.app.goo.gl` links and links on google.com all work) or the Place ID, and the code takes scanners straight to your review box. You can also add your business name, so you can find the code easily in your dashboard.

**More than one branch?** Each branch has its own Business Profile, so each needs its own review link or Place ID. A code made with the head office's link sends every review there.

## Step 3: put it where happy customers are

Timing matters more than design. Ask at the moment the customer is pleased:

- **On the bill folder or receipt** at a restaurant or cafe
- **At the reception desk** of a salon, clinic or gym, as people leave
- **On the delivery packaging** or a thank-you card in the parcel
- **On your invoice or in a WhatsApp message** after a job is done (send the review link itself in messages; the QR is for print)
- **At the checkout of a hotel or guest house,** on the key card holder or the bill
- **On a service van or the job sheet** for electricians, plumbers and repair services, handed over when the work is finished

Add a short, plain request next to the code. A few that work:

- "Enjoyed your visit? Scan to review us on Google."
- "Your review helps other people find us. Scan to leave one."
- "Aapko hamari seva kaisi lagi? Google par review dene ke liye scan karein."

Keep the code at least 2.5 cm wide on a card and bigger on a standee; our [print checklist](/blog/qr-codes-that-scan-print-checklist) covers size and contrast.

## Step 4: say it out loud

A QR code on the counter works better when a person mentions it. A simple "If you liked it, a Google review really helps us" at the end of a good visit is usually enough.

Make it part of the routine rather than a campaign: the same sentence, said by whoever hands over the bill, every day. The people who were happy will often do it there and then, while they are still waiting.

## The rules to follow

Google's [policy on fake engagement](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) is strict, and a Business Profile that breaks it repeatedly can be restricted, for example by being blocked from receiving new reviews, on top of losing the reviews themselves. In short:

- **Don't pay for reviews or offer anything in return,** such as a discount or a free item for leaving a review.
- **Don't offer anything for changing or removing a negative review** either.
- **Ask everyone, not only the customers you think are happy.** Google's policy says businesses must not discourage negative reviews or selectively ask for positive ones. Sending only satisfied customers to Google while sending unhappy ones elsewhere goes against it.
- **Don't write reviews for your own business** or ask staff and family to.

What Google does allow is asking customers to share their genuine experience, with nothing offered in return. That is exactly what a review QR code does.

If you also want private feedback, keep it separate: a [feedback form](/qr-codes/form) QR code on the table, offered to everyone, with the Google review code next to it. Don't put the form in front of the review code as a filter.

## Reply to reviews

Replies are public, and people reading reviews notice how a business responds.

- **Thank people for good reviews,** briefly and by name if they used one. Mention something specific they said.
- **Answer bad reviews calmly.** Apologise for the experience, give the facts without arguing, and offer a way to sort it out offline: a phone number or "please ask for the manager".
- **Reply within a few days.** A reply months later reads as an afterthought.
- **Never reveal private details** about a customer in a reply, even if their review is unfair.

## Measure what's working

GenXQR shows how many times each review code was scanned, by day. Your Business Profile shows how many reviews you received. Compare the two each month:

- **Lots of scans, few reviews:** people are reaching the review box but not finishing. A spoken request at the right moment usually helps more than a new design.
- **Few scans:** the code is in the wrong place or too easy to miss. Try it on the bill folder instead of the wall, or make it bigger.
- **One branch far behind the others:** compare where each branch has put its code and whether staff mention it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do customers need a Google account to leave a review?** Yes. Google asks people to sign in before posting a review, so anyone not signed in on their phone is asked to do that first.

**Will Google remove reviews that came from a QR code?** A QR code is just a shortcut to your review box. Google's rules are about how you ask (no incentives, no asking only happy customers), not about the link people used.

**What if my business name or address changes?** Scan your code afterwards to check it still opens your review box. Google can occasionally change a Place ID, so it is worth checking now and then anyway. If the link needs updating, edit the dynamic code and the printed copies keep working.

**Can one code collect reviews for several branches?** No. A code opens one business's review box. Make one code per branch, each with that branch's link.
